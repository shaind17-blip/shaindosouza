Three problems, and how I solved them.

Case 01

Turning a team of individuals into a system

Ticket Fairy · Leadership

$12.75M

team contract value against a $15M annual target

A region running on individual effort. I gave every function a number it owned and built the structure to reach it: playbook, onboarding, coaching, and the hard calls where the standard wasn't met.

Problem

The region ran on individual effort. Sales carried targets but no repeatable process; content, SEO and creative had no number they owned at all. Results swung with whoever was having a good month, and there was no way to tell a coaching problem from a hiring problem.

What I did

Gave every function a number it owned: five new logos and $20K platform revenue per BD executive per month; content output and organic traffic growth for editorial and SEO. Then I built the structure to make those numbers reachable: a sales playbook, an onboarding programme, and a commission model that paid for the behaviour I wanted.

Weekly pipeline reviews and 1:1s made performance visible early, so most conversations were coaching rather than correction. Where the standard still wasn't met after structured support, I ran the PIP and made the exit, then hired against a defined scorecard instead of instinct. Three specialist agencies were held to the same standard as internal staff.

Result

$12.75M in team contract value against a $15M annual target, with a $40M+ pipeline behind it. £300K+ delivered by the UK team and 140+ new clients in India, including a ₹20M white-label agreement. The playbook, onboarding and performance framework still run the region.

Case 02

Turning dormant signups into live events

Ticket Fairy · Platform growth

50% uplift

in self-signups going live

Organisers were signing up for the platform and never running an event. I moved the sales team onto those dormant accounts and rebuilt the process around getting each one to its first live event.

Problem

A large share of organisers who signed themselves up never went live. Accounts were created, then went quiet, so the money spent attracting them produced sign-ups instead of events, and the platform earned nothing from them.

What I did

Rather than push harder on new-logo outbound, I redeployed the sales team onto dormant account conversion and rebuilt the qualification path around getting a first event live.

Result

A 50% uplift in self-signups going live, at no additional acquisition cost, and a pipeline motion the team still runs.

Case 03

Selling into a Fortune 500 with three buyers

Green World Safety · Enterprise sales

20% YoY

growth in enterprise acquisition once the vertical opened

Bharat Petroleum was three buying groups with three different reasons to say no. I built a separate case for each and sequenced the approvals into one multi-year contract.

Problem

Bharat Petroleum is not one buyer. HSE directors were accountable for audit and compliance coverage, L&D heads for delivery and completion, procurement for price and process, and none of them could sign alone.

What I did

Ran three cases in parallel instead of one pitch: accredited programme coverage and audit trail for HSE, instructor-led delivery and enrolment tracking for L&D, and a multi-year licence with committed enrolment volumes that gave procurement predictable unit pricing.

Then I sequenced the approvals so each stakeholder was already aligned before the next conversation started, rather than letting the deal die in committee.

Result

Closed Bharat Petroleum, then repeated the structure across petroleum, manufacturing and industrial accounts including Aditya Birla, Siemens and Tata: a 20% year-on-year increase in enterprise client acquisition. Promoted to Team Lead within six months.
